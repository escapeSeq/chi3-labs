What “high volume” means here

High-volume computation is not just “a hard problem.” It is a huge number of arithmetic interactions that must happen to produce an answer. Max-Cut on N spins has a 2N configuration space and N² pairwise terms. A digital CPU issues those terms in a loop. An analog χ³ network instantiates each term as a physical product — current, optical interference, four-wave mixing — so the volume arrives in one analog instant.

  1. Represent. Numbers become continuous amplitudes xᵢ (optical field, voltage, current), not bits.
  2. Couple. The matrix–vector product Jx is done by the physics of the interconnect, not by N² multiply-adds in series.
  3. Bend with χ³. The cubic term −x³ (Kerr saturation, gain clamping) is the nonlinearity that turns a linear filter into a computer: it bounds amplitudes and selects a binary readout.
  4. Read. After a pump ramp, sign(xᵢ) is the spin. Energy has usually fallen into a deep cut without enumerating states.

The teaching model

ẋᵢ = (p − 1) xᵢ − xᵢ³ + ξ Σⱼ Jᵢⱼ xⱼ + η

This is a classroom Coherent Ising Machine. Real hardware uses degenerate optical parametric oscillators, Kerr microcombs, or analog electronics. The cubic is the fingerprint of χ³. Pump p(t) is ramped so the system first explores, then saturates.

The second pane is even more literal optics: a 2-D field diffracts (analog Fourier mixing) and then picks up a Kerr phase eiγ|E|² in a single slab. Every pixel is a multiply.

Honest limits

  • Analog precision and noise bound the bit-depth of the answer.
  • Embedding a problem onto physical couplings is itself work.
  • NP-hard problems stay NP-hard; analog volume is a throughput story, not a complexity-class collapse.
