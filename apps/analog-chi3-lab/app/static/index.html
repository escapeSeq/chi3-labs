What “high volume” means here

High-volume computation is not just “a hard problem.” It is a huge number of arithmetic interactions that must happen to produce an answer. Max-Cut on N spins has a 2N configuration space and N² pairwise terms. A digital CPU issues those terms in a loop. An analog χ³ network instantiates each term as a physical product — current, optical interference, four-wave mixing — so the volume arrives in one analog instant.

  1. Represent. Numbers become continuous amplitudes xᵢ (optical field, voltage, current), not bits.
  2. Couple. The matrix–vector product Jx is done by the physics of the interconnect, not by N² multiply-adds in series.
  3. Bend with χ³. The cubic term −x³ (Kerr saturation, gain clamping) is the nonlinearity that turns a linear filter into a computer: it bounds amplitudes and selects a binary readout.
  4. Read. After a pump ramp, sign(xᵢ) is the spin. Energy has usually fallen into a deep cut without enumerating states.

The teaching model

ẋᵢ = (p − 1) xᵢ − xᵢ³ + ξ Σⱼ Jᵢⱼ xⱼ + η

This is a classroom Coherent Ising Machine. Real hardware uses degenerate optical parametric oscillators, Kerr microcombs, or analog electronics. The cubic is the fingerprint of χ³. Pump p(t) is ramped so the system first explores, then saturates.

The second pane is even more literal optics: a 2-D field diffracts (analog Fourier mixing) and then picks up a Kerr phase eiγ|E|² in a single slab. Every pixel is a multiply.

Honest limits

  • Analog precision and noise bound the bit-depth of the answer.
  • Embedding a problem onto physical couplings is itself work.
  • NP-hard problems stay NP-hard; analog volume is a throughput story, not a complexity-class collapse.

Ising machine

A classroom Coherent Ising Machine (CIM): N analog oscillators whose amplitudes xᵢ settle into a binary pattern sᵢ = sign(xᵢ) = ±1. That pattern is a guess at a Max-Cut of the graph. A digital walker solves the same graph by flipping one spin per tick. The analog side does every pairwise product at once. 

ẋᵢ = (p − 1) xᵢ − xᵢ³ + ξ Σⱼ Jᵢⱼ xⱼ + η
  • (p − 1) xᵢ — pump / gain. Below threshold amplitudes die; as p ramps up they grow.
  • −xᵢ³ — the χ³ Kerr / saturation term. It clamps amplitudes and pushes each unit toward + or −.
  • ξ Σⱼ Jᵢⱼ xⱼ — analog coupling: every oscillator feels every other in one shot.
  • η — analog noise.

Minimizing Ising energy H = ½ sᵀ W s is the same as maximizing cut, so the analog coupling matrix is J = −W.

Oscillators N

Each node is one oscillator, not a bit. It has a continuous amplitude xᵢ (optical field, voltage, or current). Color on the graph is the readout spin sign(xᵢ); radius is |xᵢ|.

N is how many units you wire up. The search space is 2N binary assignments. The analog fabric never enumerates them: the N amplitudes evolve together. Default N = 28 is already about 268 million configurations. Brute force is only offered for N ≤ 18.

Cut

Cut is the score. Partition the nodes into two teams (+1 and −1). An edge is cut when its two ends are on different teams: 

cut = ¼ Σᵢⱼ Wᵢⱼ (1 − sᵢ sⱼ)

Opposite signs (sᵢ sⱼ = −1) add the edge weight. Same signs add nothing. Cut ceiling is “every edge cut,” Σi<j wᵢⱼ — often impossible, because cutting one edge can force another not to be cut.

Cyan edges are currently cut; red edges are uncut. The oscilloscope plots cut vs effort. Higher cut is better. Analog best cut and digital best cut are the highest values each solver reached on this run.

Couplings / instant

A coupling is one pairwise product Jᵢⱼ xⱼ — how much oscillator j pulls oscillator i. Digitally that is an N×N matrix–vector multiply, issued in a loop. In the analog story, the interconnect is that multiply: every product happens in the same physical moment.

An instant is one analog time sample of the ODE (one Euler step in this simulator). In that instant all N oscillators update, all N² couplings Jx fire, plus a few local χ³ ops per oscillator.

Analog couplings / instant is therefore N². Equivalent serial analog ops is that volume times the number of analog steps (~420). Digital updates is how many single-spin flips the walker got in the same run. NP-hard stays NP-hard; analog media can dump a huge number of products into one physical instant.

Analog χ³ knobs

These are the analog ODE parameters, not the graph.

Pump ramp end p
How hard you drive the oscillators. Pump starts negative (−0.55) and ramps to this value. Too low: they never saturate. Too high: they slam to ± before the couplings can organize a good cut.
Coupling ξ
How strongly each oscillator feels Jx. Too small: units ignore the graph. Too large: the analog state can overshoot or lock into a mediocre pattern. Default 0.22.
Analog noise η
Brownian kick each instant. A little noise helps escape shallow cuts. Too much, and the readout jitters and the cut suffers.

Relax

Relax both machines means: let the analog network run its pump ramp, and let the digital walker take its sequential steps, then compare cuts.

Analog relaxation is physical settling. Early in the ramp (p low), amplitudes are small and noisy — exploration. As pump rises, χ³ saturation locks each unit toward ±, and the couplings pull the whole pattern toward a deep cut. You read the answer; you do not search the 2N list.

Digital methods are not relaxing in that sense. Metropolis tries one flip per tick and may accept a worse cut (simulated annealing). Greedy only accepts an improving flip. Brute enumerates, with global-flip symmetry removed.

Seed

Seed is the random-number seed that builds the graph (which edges exist, their weights, planted communities) and also offsets the analog and digital initial conditions.

Same Problem + N + Seed draws the same instance, so analog vs digital is a fair fight. Change the seed to draw a new random graph of that kind. Analog starts from seed+11 and the digital walker from seed+3, so they share the problem, not the exact same initial draw.

How to read a run

  1. Pick a problem and N, optionally a seed.
  2. Open Analog χ³ knobs if you want to change pump, coupling, or noise.
  3. Hit Relax both machines.
  4. Watch analog nodes grow and flip color as they saturate; digital nodes flip one at a time.
  5. Compare Analog best cut vs Digital best cut vs Cut ceiling.
  6. Volume meters tell you how much arithmetic happened: analog paid N² couplings every instant; digital paid one spin flip per tick.

The graph is the problem, oscillators are the hardware, couplings are the all-at-once multiply, cut is the score, knobs are the analog physics, seed is which random instance, and relax is “run until the analog fabric settles.”
