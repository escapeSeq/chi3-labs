What a nonlinear analog medium actually does

Before an analog computer can multiply, mix, or lock a spin, a field has to enter a medium. Linear media copy. Nonlinear media multiply: χ² squares the field, χ³ cubes it. This lab is that constitutive curve, live on a scope — the physics the analog computing lab then puts to work.

Three facts, then a computer

Analog physics is continuous fields evolving in a medium. Digital physics is bits updated in a loop. The whole χ³ computing story sits on three facts you can see on the Drive pane.

  1. Linear copies. P = χ¹ E. Superposition holds. The spectrum of the output is the spectrum of the input. A linear analog box is a filter, not a computer.
  2. Powers multiply. E² and E³ are analog multiplies that happen at every instant, at every point in the medium. New frequencies are the receipt.
  3. A cubic can saturate. Gain plus −χ³ x³ splits a well into ±. That is a bit written by physics, not by a comparison instruction.

The analog lab then wires many of those wells together with a coupling matrix and calls the settled signs a Max-Cut. This lab is the single well, the single multiply, the single constitutive curve.

The teaching model

P = χ⁽¹⁾ E + χ⁽²⁾ E² + χ⁽³⁾ E³

Instantaneous, weakly nonlinear, no dispersion. A sine through χ² becomes DC + 2ω because sin²ωt = ½ − ½ cos 2ωt. A sine through χ³ becomes a corrected ω plus 3ω because sin³ωt = ¾ sin ωt − ¼ sin 3ωt. Two sines through either are analog mixers. 

n = n₀ + n₂ I     ẋ = (p − 1) x − χ³ x³

Same cubic, two costumes: a Kerr phase on a beam, and a Duffing well that can lock. Real hardware uses crystals, fibers, transistors, Josephson junctions — all of them a P(E) curve.

Honest limits

  • No memory, no delay, no noise figure — those are the next labs.
  • χ² needs a medium without inversion symmetry. χ³ is allowed everywhere.
  • Saturation makes a bit; it does not by itself solve a hard graph.

Medium

Whatever writes a response P from a field E: an optical crystal, a Kerr fiber, a transistor’s transfer curve, a Josephson junction. The lab treats it as a function P(E) applied to the whole waveform at once — analog, not a loop over samples.

Linear / χ¹

P = χ¹ E. Output is a scaled copy of input. Superposition holds: two drives stay two drives. The spectrum does not grow new lines. A linear analog box filters; it does not mix or compute. 

P = χ⁽¹⁾ E

Quadratic / χ²

An E² term. Even in the field: flipping E does not flip P. One sine yields DC (optical rectification) and a second harmonic. Two sines yield the sum and the difference — a mixer, which is analog multiplication in frequency. 

sin² ωt = ½ − ½ cos 2ωt

Sign of χ² tilts the parabola on the constitutive plot.

Cubic / χ³

An E³ term. Odd in the field. One sine yields a third harmonic plus an intensity-dependent correction at ω (the Kerr index). Two sines yield four-wave mixing 2ω₁−ω₂. Negative χ³ on the Drive pane saturates the S-curve; that is the same cubic the saturating well uses as a restoring force. 

sin³ ωt = ¾ sin ωt − ¼ sin 3ωt

Superposition

P(E₁ + E₂) = P(E₁) + P(E₂). True if and only if the medium is linear. The meter is the normalized gap. When it leaves zero, the medium is multiplying the field by itself, and new frequencies are the receipt.

Kerr index

n = n₀ + n₂ I with I = |E|². Intensity writes phase. A bright core advances or retards relative to the wings — self-phase modulation. It is χ³ looking at space: every point of the beam is an analog multiply, in one optical instant. 

n = n₀ + n₂ |E|²

Saturating well

Overdamped Duffing, the classroom CIM oscillator with the graph unplugged. Below threshold (p < 1) everything dies to 0. Above threshold, gain without a cubic runs away; gain with a cubic splits the origin into ±√((p−1)/χ³). sign(x) after settling is a bit written by physics. 

ẋ = (p − 1) x − χ³ x³
