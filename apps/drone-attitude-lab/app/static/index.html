Why a drone needs four numbers

A quadrotor has one force it can steer: the sum of its rotor thrusts, pointed down the body z axis. Gravity points down the world z axis. Every manoeuvre is a choice of how hard to push and which way the belly faces.

  1. Attitude is a rotation. It maps body vectors (thrust, rotor positions) into the world. R · [0, 0, −T] is the force that actually moves the drone.
  2. Euler angles are a recipe. Yaw, then pitch, then roll about moving axes. Readable, but the recipe has a singularity: at pitch ±90° roll and yaw do the same thing.
  3. Quaternions are a place. Four numbers on a unit sphere, no singular attitude, composition is a product, and the rate equation q̇ = ½ q ⊗ ω has no trig or division.
  4. Rotation vectors are the bridge. Axis × angle in three numbers. Body rates ω are rotation vectors per second, and exp(ω dt / 2) is the quaternion step.

The control loop in one breath

Want to go north? Need a north force. Thrust must lean north, so command a nose-down pitch. The attitude error between where the belly points and where it should point is a rotation vector; the controller turns it into body torques; the mixer turns torques into four rotor thrusts. Then increase the total so T cos tilt still equals mg.

Conventions

  • World NED, body FRD, as in PX4 and most aerospace texts.
  • Hamilton quaternions, scalar first, body → world.
  • Positive roll drops the right arm, positive pitch lifts the nose, positive yaw turns right.

Honest limits

  • Rigid body. No drag on the frame, no ground effect, no motor lag.
  • Rotor thrust is clipped to 0–9 N; yaw torque is 0.016 m × thrust.
  • The force tab is a snapshot: it shows the accelerations, not the flight that follows.

World and body frames

World NED: x north, y east, z down, fixed to the ground. Body FRD: x out the nose, y out the right arm, z out the belly, bolted to the drone. Thrust is simple in the body ([0, 0, −T]); gravity is simple in the world ([0, 0, mg]). The rotation R converts.

Euler angles

Three successive turns about named axes. Aerospace uses intrinsic Z-Y-X: yaw ψ about z, then pitch θ about the new y, then roll φ about the newest x. Change the order and the same numbers point the drone somewhere else. 

R = Rz(ψ) Ry(θ) Rx(φ)

Gimbal lock

At pitch ±90° the roll axis lines up with the yaw axis. Roll and yaw now produce the same motion, and only φ − ψ (or φ + ψ) is defined. The drone has not lost a degree of freedom — the angles have. Near the lock, W(φ, θ) divides by cos θ and the Euler rates needed for an ordinary body rate run off to infinity.

Quaternion

q = w + xi + yj + zk with |q| = 1. For a turn of α about unit axis n̂, q = [cos(α/2), sin(α/2) n̂]. Composing turns is the Hamilton product. q and −q are the same attitude (double cover), so a 360° turn gives −q and 720° gets back to q. 

q̇ = ½ q ⊗ [0, ω]

Rotation vector

r = α n̂: direction is the axis, length is the angle. Every attitude is one turn about some fixed axis (Euler's rotation theorem); this is that turn in three numbers. Attitude errors in controllers are usually rotation vectors. 

q = [cos(|r|/2), sin(|r|/2) r/|r|]

Rotation matrix

Nine numbers, orthonormal, det = +1. Column i is body axis i written in world coordinates. Multiplying by R is the cheapest way to rotate many vectors; nine numbers for three degrees of freedom is the price.

Angular velocity ω

A vector whose direction is the instantaneous spin axis and whose length is the rate. Gyros measure it in the body frame as (p, q, r). It is not (φ̇, θ̇, ψ̇) — those are related by W(φ, θ), which is singular at pitch ±90°.

Thrust vector

Rotors only push along −z body. Tilt by θ and the vertical share is T cos θ, the horizontal share T sin θ. Hold altitude while tilted and T must rise to mg / cos θ — that is why a fast drone runs out of throttle.

Torque and mixing

Roll torque: τx = Σ −yᵢ Fᵢ (left rotors up, roll right). Pitch: τy = Σ xᵢ Fᵢ (front rotors up, nose up). Yaw comes from prop drag: CCW props drag the frame clockwise. The mixer inverts that 4 × 4 map: from [T, τx, τy, τz] to four rotor thrusts.

SLERP

Spherical linear interpolation between unit quaternions: constant angular rate about one fixed axis, the shortest turn between two attitudes. Pick the sign so q₀ · q₁ ≥ 0, or it goes the long way round. 

slerp = sin((1−t)Ω)/sin Ω · q₀ + sin(tΩ)/sin Ω · q₁
