Chi3-sim · aerial gunnery academy

Two empty brains, one turn-radius gun fight

Each plane is a Dubins vehicle: it cannot spin in place. Yaw is capped by speed ÷ turn radius, and the gun is welded to the nose. Both policies start as random weights — no tactics. Sorties run continuously: when one fight ends, the next starts on its own, and each one is another trial-and-error update. Name the brains, assign them to seats, and freeze any net you do not want updated. Weights are stored on /data so a restart keeps what they learned.

Why the turn radius matters

Instant heading changes would make this a pointing contest. A tight radius means every shot is bought with a curve. The dashed circles on the field are the only paths the plane is allowed to follow.

Why the gun only fires forward

Bullets inherit the nose heading and never steer. Getting a kill is geometry: be behind, or cut across the circle, then squeeze. Firing sideways does nothing — the net has to discover that.

Trial and error

After each sortie the policy gets a return: plus for a kill and for pointing the nose, minus for walls and dying. REINFORCE raises the probability of the action sequence that produced a better return. That is all “learning” is here.
