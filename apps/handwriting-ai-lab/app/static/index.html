How a net learns handwriting — then how it uses what it learned

Training is a lesson: labeled pages go in, the net guesses, a loss scores the miss, and weights are nudged. Using is a frozen forward pass: you write, pixels flow through those weights, and nothing is rewritten. Write with the mouse on the pad.

    What the net actually sees

    Your stroke is not stored as a story. It is crushed to a 16×16 grid of numbers. That grid is the only input.

    1. Pixels become a vector of 256 numbers.
    2. Each hidden neuron scores “does this look like my template?”
    3. Ten output scores become a guess 0–9. Weights stay put.

    Training vs using

    A lesson walks all five steps, including the red ones: compare, blame, nudge. Asking the net does only the forward guess. The weights stay put — that is using, not learning.

    How to read the weights

    Cyan in a template means “ink here helps this neuron fire.” Red means “ink here is evidence against.” After ReLU, a negative score is silence, and a silent unit cannot vote. The vote grid is the second layer: cyan raises a digit, red suppresses it. A lesson writes these numbers onto disk at /data; a restart reloads them.
